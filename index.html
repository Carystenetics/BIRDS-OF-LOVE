a poem about love, told by birds

The Way Birds Love

Some things about love make more sense mid-flight — the nest built one twig at a time, the storms flown through together, the way home that's never really lost.

I. The Nest

Some birds spend a whole spring
weaving a single nest from nothing

a twig at a time, a promise at a time,
until it finally holds.

II. The First Flight

Falling in love, someone once said,
feels a lot like falling —

until you notice neither of us
ever hit the ground.

We just… flew.

III. The Storm

Even birds get caught
in storms they didn't see coming.

They don't leave the sky because of it.

They just fly closer together.

IV. Migration

Some birds travel thousands of miles
and somehow still find their way back
to the exact same branch.

I don't know how they do it.

I only know I'd find my way back to you.

V. The Dawn Chorus

Every morning, before the world is fully awake,
the birds start singing anyway —

not because anyone's listening,
but because the sun came up,
and that alone is worth singing about.

I choose you the same way.

VI. Coming Home

They say some birds mate for life —
not because they can't leave,
but because they keep choosing not to.

Every year, the same sky.
Every year, the same branch.
Every year, still you.

Wherever I fly, I'm already flying home.
